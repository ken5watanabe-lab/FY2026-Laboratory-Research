## Research Project; Regional differences in type 2 diabetes prevalence in Japan
abstract
Background and Objective:
 Regional differences in type 2 diabetes prevalence have been reported in Japan, but the underlying factors remain unclear. This study investigated whether genetic risk, neighborhood environment, and physical activity could explain these regional differences.

Methods:
 A cross-sectional analysis was conducted among 13,653 participants in the J-MICC Study. Associations of polygenic risk score (PRS), Japan Postcode Walkability Index (JPWI), and physical activity with type 2 diabetes were evaluated using logistic regression analyses. Interactions with study region and among explanatory factors were also examined.

Results:
 PRS was consistently associated with type 2 diabetes across all study regions. However, five regions showed lower diabetes prevalence residuals than expected from PRS residuals. JPWI showed a significant interaction with study region, but no significant association was observed in region-specific analyses. Physical activity was not associated with type 2 diabetes. No significant interactions were observed among PRS, JPWI, and physical activity.

Conclusion:
 Although genetic risk was consistently associated with type 2 diabetes, regional differences could not be fully explained by PRS alone. Factors other than genetic susceptibility may contribute to regional variation in diabetes prevalence.

Future Perspectives:
 Further studies incorporating dietary habits and additional environmental factors are warranted.
